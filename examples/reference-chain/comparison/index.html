Conclusions

  • Every device here is the built-in reference chain. This is the measurement floor, not a result for a product: the built-in reference chain reads SINAD 147.1 dB and dynamic range 151.7 dB (A-weighted) at DSD64. A DSD to PCM converter within 6 dB of these figures (SINAD above 141.1 dB) cannot be ranked by this tool.
  • The parts of the chain agree: SINAD is 146.9 dB for reference decoder and 146.9 dB for reference modulator, a spread of less than 0.01 dB.
  • Not compared in this version: filter and ringing, polarity, multitone, J-Test, peak-level SINAD and modulator stability. These figures are not validated yet or not used by any rule, and converters often differ most in their filters and ultrasonic noise, so this comparison is not the whole picture.

Levels: dBFS is relative to PCM full scale. 0 dB SACD is the reference tone level, 50 % modulation, -6.02 dBFS; noise in dBFS is referred to the rms of a full-scale sine, as in AES17. Distortion and noise figures are in dB below the 1 kHz tone unless they say dBFS. Bandwidth 20 Hz to 20 kHz, unweighted unless marked A-weighted. SINAD is THD+N turned positive. Because the tone is 6 dB below full scale, a noise-limited device would read about 6 dB higher at full scale; compare reports made at the same level. These are not AES17 measurements: only the 20 kHz band edge and the dBFS convention follow it; the tone (1 kHz, not 997 Hz), the weighting (A, not CCIR-RMS), the notch, the level and the dither differ (docs/methodology-limits.md). No cell is marked: no repeatability interval has been measured yet, so no difference is called an improvement. 'floor' marks a figure within 6 dB of that device's reference floor, so it may be limited by the measurement.
